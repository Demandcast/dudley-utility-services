01 · How this works

One system, every voice

What this section is: The rules every post inherits

Most pieces will exist twice, written once from the company and once as Garrett, with Savannah joining as her voice gets established. The standing rules come from your own feedback and apply to every post automatically.

The four pillars come from the Dudley values: people, service, product, example. Each carries a full-thought title, so anyone can tell where a piece of content belongs by the title alone.

Industries are a dimension

A post is "How the work works, in electric transmission." Industries flavor the pillars; they never become extra ones.

One piece, every voice

Each piece gets written from the company voice, then tailored to Garrett. Savannah joins the same pattern once her voice is established.

The byline test

If the named person would read the post and think "that didn't happen to me," it doesn't ship in their voice.

Standing rules on every post. The brand voice guide governs. The 45 years belongs to Dudley Land Company by name. No client, project, route, or location names. Our readers do this work daily, so nothing we publish explains their own job to them.
Added from your Aug 7 feedback. Condemnation and eminent domain never lead: a capability we can state mid-sentence, never a headline, never prominent where landowners will read it. The words: right-of-way, never corridor; client or utility company, never buyer; gas utility, never gas pipeline; secured, never cleared. Pillar one · The People

The people on the project are the project

What this section is: The first pillar, and how to read all four

Every pillar section on this page has the same three parts: the idea in plain terms (the paragraph below), the kind of material it builds up over time (the green-edged box), and a numbered list of example post topics. Those are subjects we'd write about under this pillar. Each one becomes real drafts in several formats: plain text posts, a text-plus-graphic, a card-style carousel you swipe through, and a short video script.

The people on a project determine how the project goes. We invest in the people who do the work: cross-training land managers across every step, staffing right-of-way agents local to the ground they walk, and recruiting through an engine built since 1980. That investment is what the client actually feels.

What it produces: the culture-and-craft record. What a utility land career at Dudley looks like, what cross-training means in practice, and why the person at the landowner's door matters to the schedule.

Pillar three · The Product

Posts about the problems a right-of-way throws and how Dudley handles them. Readers who do this work daily judge a company by whether it talks about the work the way people who do it talk about it. We have ample material to get started here, so it's the first set we'll write.

Pillar four · The Example

The work is the proof

What this section is: The receipts pillar

Real outcomes, told as they happened, anonymized. It starts small on purpose: nothing here publishes until the figures you're compiling arrive verified in writing, and any story told in your name gets your sign-off first.

Demonstrate expertise, never claim it. Real business development is delivering on time and in budget, so posts under this pillar are receipts: outcomes held, challenges closed, case studies as our work completes, always anonymized.
