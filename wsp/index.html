Executive Summary

Dudley Utility Services (DUS) is pleased to present the experience and resources we bring to utility land projects across the contiguous 48 states. Our utility practice combines Dudley’s established land services foundation with experienced leadership and a national network of land professionals to support your projects from early development through construction and closeout.

Founded in 1980 by Tom Dudley, Dudley brings more than 45 years of land services experience, courthouse knowledge, and established landowner relationships. Our dedicated utility division builds on that foundation with a W-2 workforce of land professionals, supported by an extensive recruiting network and proven processes for mobilizing and scaling teams to meet project demands. Our services extend from early siting and ownership research through survey access, title curative, easement and fee acquisition, permitting, construction liaison, and closeout.

Our utility leadership team’s experience encompasses more than 10,000 miles of transmission, distribution, and fiber infrastructure for more than 40 utility and public power clients, including electric transmission projects up to 765 kV. We bring that experience to each assignment through realistic planning, consistent landowner communication, and disciplined project oversight. Clients receive one accountable partner and a single point of contact, with clear expectations for staffing, cost, and schedule throughout the project lifecycle.

Two examples from our leadership team’s prior utility programs are included as appendices. Appendix A (Duke Energy) demonstrates rapid workforce mobilization, and Appendix B (AEP) demonstrates sustained program growth and concurrent project execution.

Ramping and Scaling Capabilities

Dudley scales project teams to respond quickly to changing workloads, accelerated schedules, and evolving project requirements. Our active workforce, bench network, and dedicated recruiting department provide the capacity to expand teams as demand increases and reduce staffing efficiently as work is completed. Staffing decisions are coordinated with the client and aligned with project milestones, available work, and budget expectations.

Ramp-Up Strategy

Our recruiting department works with project leadership to anticipate staffing needs, identify qualified land professionals, and confirm availability before additional resources are required. Regular contact with our bench network, combined with ongoing recruiting efforts, allows us to prepare for planned mobilization and respond to unexpected increases in scope or schedule demands. Screening, onboarding, and required training are coordinated ahead of deployment to shorten the time between identifying a need and placing professionals on the project.

We establish a core leadership and support team at mobilization, then expand staffing as project activities and workload require. Project leadership reviews upcoming milestones, work releases, productivity, and outstanding assignments to determine the appropriate timing and size of each staffing increase. When demand accelerates, we coordinate available personnel from existing assignments, our bench network, and recruiting efforts to increase capacity while maintaining supervision, quality, and continuity. Our leadership team has applied this approach at scale, ramping a multi-state utility program to 130 Land Representatives within approximately 60 days (see Appendix A).

Ramp-Down Strategy

Staffing reductions are planned around remaining workload and completion milestones. As assignments are completed, project leadership coordinates with the client to release or redeploy personnel in stages, keeping staffing and cost aligned with the work still required. We retain the leadership and specialized resources needed to support unresolved parcels, final payments, construction coordination, and closeout.

Before personnel transition, open assignments are documented and transferred, files are reviewed, and landowner commitments are captured. Recruiting and operations coordinate upcoming assignments to support timely redeployment where opportunities are available. This process allows Dudley to reduce staffing efficiently while preserving project knowledge, maintaining accountability, and supporting an orderly closeout.

Quality Control

Every deliverable receives a documented second-level review before release to the client. Acquisition documents undergo three stages of review: before landowner presentation, during negotiation and execution, and before recording. These reviews confirm ownership information against title records, verify exhibits and legal descriptions, and check packet completeness, signatures, and notarization.

Landowner contacts are logged in KUDU and the client’s database where applicable, with supervisory approval before inclusion in the permanent record. Certified easement exhibits are checked for landowner names, applicable legal location references, acreage, configuration, and excluded conveyances. Executed originals are submitted to the client within 24 hours of signing. A designated Project Manager serves as the client’s single point of contact for each project or region.

Project Controls and Reporting

The controls below are designed to scale from a single project to a multi-project program. Appendix B describes how our leadership team applied them across a portfolio that grew to approximately 160 utility projects over three years.

  • Weekly Reporting. Acquisition and construction restriction line lists covering every tract, parcel counts, and milestone status across title, survey, acquisition, and construction support, along with Cost Performance Index (CPI) and Schedule Performance Index (SPI) tracking.
  • Monthly Forecasting. Forecasts of costs at completion compared with approved budget baselines, including applicable right-of-way and construction support labor, acquisition payments, and damages.
  • Risk and Change Management. A weekly issues and risk log with Critical, High, Medium, or Low ratings, plus a change log documenting cost, schedule, and scope impacts, the client’s disposition, and required actions. Both logs are included in weekly client reporting.
  • Landowner Provisions. Conditions relating to construction, encroachments, environmental matters, forestry, document language, permits, access, and survey restrictions are recorded in KUDU and the client’s database where applicable, carrying commitments from negotiation into construction.
  • Landowner Sentiment and Access. KUDU identifies tracts as Clear, Concern, Caution, Do Not Contact, No Entry, or Safety Concern, giving project personnel visibility into communication sensitivities, access limitations, and situations requiring additional coordination.

Appendix A: Rapid Resource Mobilization & Workforce Development

Duke Energy | Targeted Underground Program
Prior Employer Experience – Utility Leadership Team
Capability demonstrated: rapid workforce mobilization

Program Overview

Our utility leadership team previously led the transition and stabilization of Duke Energy’s Targeted Underground Program while employed by a prior organization. The assignment involved assuming responsibility for a multi-state right-of-way program from an underperforming incumbent provider whose existing staffing levels were insufficient to support the volume of work and project delivery expectations.

The transition required considerably more than replacing the incumbent workforce. It demanded an accelerated mobilization strategy capable of significantly expanding field resources while maintaining continuity of active right-of-way acquisition activities across multiple states and operating territories.

Within approximately 60 days, the leadership team successfully ramped field resources to 130 Land Representatives, implementing a coordinated recruitment, onboarding, training, and deployment strategy. Rather than mobilizing resources without adequate preparation, the team established a structured training program that allowed personnel to be recruited, trained, and deployed in successive waves while maintaining consistency in execution and adherence to client requirements.

Structured 60-Day Mobilization

The workforce expansion was supported by a coordinated approach integrating resource planning, targeted recruitment, leadership oversight, and regional deployment. Working directly with utility program management, the leadership team evaluated staffing requirements across the operating territories and established a mobilization strategy aligned with anticipated workloads and existing project priorities.

Training was a critical component of the transition. An organized curriculum was developed and delivered through multiple scheduled sessions throughout the mobilization period. The program included utility-specific processes and procedures, safety requirements, power delivery fundamentals, design interpretation, right-of-way acquisition practices, public engagement, landowner communication, document quality assurance, and client-specific technology platforms.

Experienced personnel were strategically integrated into the training process to facilitate knowledge transfer, provide field mentoring, and accelerate the development of newly recruited Land Representatives. Training sessions combined classroom instruction, practical application, scenario-based exercises, and field shadowing to reinforce operational readiness before independent deployment.

This coordinated approach allowed the leadership team to progressively increase field capacity while reinforcing consistent execution standards, quality expectations, and accountability across the program. As additional personnel completed onboarding and training, resources were strategically assigned to regional teams based on project demands, allowing the organization to expand without unnecessarily disrupting ongoing acquisition activities.

Program Overview

Our utility leadership team previously managed the expansion and execution of a multi-state utility infrastructure portfolio supporting American Electric Power (AEP) while employed by a prior organization. Unlike a single large-scale project, this assignment required managing numerous projects simultaneously, each with varying scopes, schedules, geographic locations, resource requirements, and stages of development. The leadership team established a scalable operational framework capable of supporting new project awards while maintaining execution commitments across the existing portfolio.

As the program expanded, resources were strategically deployed and reassigned based on project priorities, anticipated workloads, and individual project schedules. Three-Year Project Scale-Up

The portfolio expansion required a structured approach to planning, mobilization, and operational oversight. Rather than building independent teams for every new assignment, the leadership team developed a coordinated program-management structure that could support multiple projects simultaneously, leveraging shared leadership, administrative functions, and specialized right-of-way resources.

As additional projects entered the portfolio, workforce requirements were evaluated against anticipated project schedules, acquisition activities, and geographic considerations. Recruiting and deployment strategies were adjusted as necessary to support project initiation, maintain existing commitments, and reduce gaps between project assignments.

Resource Planning & Workforce Scalability

The growth of the AEP portfolio required a flexible workforce strategy that could respond to changing project demands without compromising ongoing execution. Leadership continuously evaluated upcoming project awards, anticipated notice-to-proceed dates, required field resources, and the specialized capabilities needed to support individual assignments.

As workload increased, personnel were mobilized through targeted recruitment, internal resource allocation, and the reassignment of experienced professionals as project phases progressed. This approach supported efficient workforce utilization while providing continuity in project leadership and client relationships.

Regional management and project leadership coordinated staffing requirements across the portfolio, enabling resources to be shared where appropriate and allowing additional personnel to be introduced as project activity increased.

Standardized Project Execution & Controls

Supporting a portfolio of this magnitude required more than workforce availability. It demanded consistent project controls, clearly defined responsibilities, reliable reporting, and proactive coordination across multiple active assignments.

The leadership team applied a program-level management approach to align project execution with client expectations while allowing individual project teams to address local requirements, landowner considerations, and project-specific challenges.

Key management priorities included workforce forecasting, project scheduling, right-of-way acquisition planning, budget oversight, production tracking, quality assurance, and regular communication with AEP project stakeholders.

This structure provided leadership with portfolio-wide visibility while preserving accountability at the individual project level.

Managing Concurrent Project Demands

As the number of active projects increased, resource planning became increasingly important. Projects progressed through different phases of the land and right-of-way lifecycle, creating fluctuating demands for title research, survey coordination, acquisition, permitting support, and construction liaison activities.

The leadership team coordinated personnel across these phases to support project schedules while limiting unnecessary workforce disruptions. By monitoring anticipated workload changes and maintaining communication between project managers and field leadership, the organization could respond to shifting priorities and support additional assignments as they were released.

This disciplined approach to concurrent project delivery helped establish a scalable management framework capable of supporting both individual project requirements and the broader portfolio.
